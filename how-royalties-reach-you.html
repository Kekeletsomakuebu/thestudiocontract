How Royalties Actually Reach You

Streams and plays add up, but the money only reaches you if the right information is registered in the right places.

A lot of creators release music, see streams and plays add up, and then wonder why no money ever seems to land in their pocket. Usually it's not that the money doesn't exist — it's that nobody registered the right information for it to find its way to them.

The three royalty types you'll actually run into

Mechanical royalties

Paid to whoever owns the composition, whenever the song is reproduced — streamed, downloaded, pressed onto a CD. On streaming platforms this is usually bundled into what the platform pays out per stream.

Performance royalties

Paid whenever a song is performed publicly — played on the radio, in a club, at an event, or broadcast on TV. This is where collection societies do most of their work: they license venues and broadcasters, then distribute the money back to the songwriters and publishers whose music got played.

Sync royalties

Paid when your music is used alongside video — a film, an ad, a YouTube video, a TV show. These are usually negotiated directly as one-off fees rather than collected automatically.

Where collection societies fit in

Collecting societies exist because it would be impossible for you personally to track every radio station, club, and shopping centre playing your music and invoice each one. Instead, a society issues blanket licences to those music users, collects the fees, and distributes them back to registered members based on what got played.

In Lesotho, that role belongs to LESCOSAA — the Lesotho Copyright Society of Authors and Artistes. It exists specifically to collect and distribute royalties owed to Basotho songwriters, composers, and rights holders when their work is used.

The part people miss: collection societies can only pay you if you're registered with them and your songs are properly logged with accurate writer and ownership information. Unregistered plays are money that has nowhere to go.

Why you might not be getting paid

  • You're not registered with a collection society at all.
  • Your song's metadata (writer names, splits, ISRC/ISWC codes) is missing or wrong on the platforms it's distributed to.
  • You released independently through a platform like BeatStars or YouTube without setting up publishing administration, so performance royalties have nowhere to be tracked back to you.
  • You collaborated with someone and never agreed on a split sheet, so royalties can't be divided correctly — or get held up entirely.

What to actually do

  1. Register with LESCOSAA as a songwriter/rights holder, and make sure your catalogue is logged with them.
  2. Always agree on a split sheet with collaborators before or right after a session — who wrote what percentage, in writing.
  3. Double check your metadata is correct wherever you distribute — especially writer credits — since wrong data is one of the most common reasons royalties go unpaid.

This guide is educational, not legal advice. Copyright and royalty rules can change, and your specific situation may differ — for anything that affects a real deal or registration, confirm details directly with LESCOSAA or a lawyer.
